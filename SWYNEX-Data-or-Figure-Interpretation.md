# SWYNEX – Data or Figure Interpretation

## Title
**Interpretation of Published Clinical Trial Data of the Pfizer–BioNTech mRNA COVID-19 Vaccine**

## 1. Introduction
Messenger RNA (mRNA) vaccines provide cells with instructions to produce a target antigen that stimulates an immune response. This report interprets published Phase 3 clinical trial data for the Pfizer–BioNTech BNT162b2 COVID-19 vaccine.

## 2. Objective
To interpret published clinical trial data, understand the comparison between vaccine and placebo groups, and identify conclusions supported by the results.

## 3. Data Source
Polack FP et al. (2020). *Safety and Efficacy of the BNT162b2 mRNA Covid-19 Vaccine.* The New England Journal of Medicine.

DOI: https://doi.org/10.1056/NEJMoa2034577

## 4. Methodology
The original study was a randomized, placebo-controlled, observer-blinded Phase 3 clinical trial. Participants were assigned to receive either the BNT162b2 vaccine or a placebo. Researchers compared confirmed COVID-19 cases between the groups after the specified vaccination period.

This repository contains a secondary interpretation of published results. No laboratory experiments or independent clinical testing were conducted.

## 5. Published Data

| Parameter | Vaccine group | Placebo group |
|---|---:|---:|
| Participants in primary efficacy analysis | 18,198 | 18,325 |
| Confirmed COVID-19 cases | 8 | 162 |

The reported vaccine efficacy was approximately 95%, measured from seven days after the second dose in the primary efficacy analysis.

## 6. Data Interpretation
The placebo group had substantially more confirmed COVID-19 cases than the vaccine group. The difference supports the conclusion that BNT162b2 provided strong protection against symptomatic COVID-19 under the conditions of this clinical trial.

Vaccine efficacy is a relative measure of risk reduction between study groups. It does not mean that every vaccinated individual has exactly 95% protection.

## 7. Conclusions
The published data demonstrated high efficacy of the BNT162b2 mRNA vaccine against symptomatic COVID-19 during the study period. The findings supported further evaluation and authorization of the vaccine.

The results should be interpreted within the study's population, follow-up period, and original conditions. They do not independently establish long-term protection, effectiveness against every later variant, or complete prevention of infection.

## 8. Limitations
- This report relies on published secondary data.
- The findings relate to the original clinical trial period and study population.
- The number of confirmed cases alone does not describe every aspect of vaccine safety.
- Longer-term effectiveness requires additional evidence.
